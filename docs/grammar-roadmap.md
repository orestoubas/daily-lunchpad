# Grammar section — roadmap

Twenty grammar topics were proposed to Orestis on 26 August 2026. He chose to
build the five with the highest payoff first and keep the rest as a backlog.
**This file is the backlog. Work the "Later" list in order when adding more.**

Learner: A2 working level, target B2, EPSO AD5 competition, lives in Brussels.

## Why these five

The existing banks already *drill* grammar — 180 grammar items, 120
conjugation, 150 prepositions — but nothing *teaches* a topic end to end. The
Grammar section is the taught version. The five chosen are the ones that either
compound (an error repeats in every later sentence), block the jump from B1 to
B2, or convert directly into exam marks.

| # | Topic | Levels | Why it was picked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gender and plural of nouns | A2 · B1 | Highest leverage at A2. A wrong gender propagates into every article, adjective and pronoun that follows, so fixing it now removes errors he has not yet made. |
| 2 | Articles and partitives | A2 · B1 | `le / du / de la / des`, `pas de`, `beaucoup de`. The most frequent persistent English-speaker error, and it appears in essentially every sentence. |
| 3 | Object pronouns — COD, COI, `y`, `en`, and order | B1 · B2 | The single biggest wall between B1 and B2. Currently only 10 drill items exist. |
| 4 | Relative pronouns — `qui / que / dont / où`, `lequel` | B1 · B2 | You cannot write a B2 sentence without `dont`. Currently only 8 drill items. |
| 5 | Connectors and argument structure | B1 · B2 | `cependant`, `néanmoins`, `en revanche`, `or`, `ainsi`, `dans la mesure où`. Covered nowhere, and worth marks directly in the EUFTE essay. |

`passé composé vs imparfait` was deliberately left out of the first five despite
being important: at 30 items it is already the best-covered topic in the drill
bank, so it buys less than the five above.

## Later — in suggested order

Each entry keeps the number it had in the original proposal.

**Next up (the rest of the recommended ten)**

- **8. Passé composé vs imparfait** — plus past-participle agreement with `avoir`
  and `être`, and with a preceding direct object. *30 drill items already exist.*
- **10. Si clauses** — the three patterns. A recognised B1→B2 gate.
- **17. Asking questions in three registers** — intonation, `est-ce que`,
  inversion. Inversion is required for anything formal.
- **1. Numbers, prices and quantities** — Belgian `septante`/`nonante`, decimal
  comma, percentages, `un million de`. Daily use plus numerical reasoning.
- **3. Dates, duration and frequency** — `depuis` / `pendant` / `il y a` /
  `dans` / `en`, `le 3 mai`, `en 2026`. *Partly covered by the preposition bank's
  30 time items.*

**Then**

- **19. Reported speech and sequence of tenses** — turning what was said into a
  note. This is close to the AD5 note-writing task and should be promoted if
  exam practice becomes the priority.
- **20. Administrative and formal French** — nominalisation, the passive and how
  French avoids it (`on`, `se faire`, pronominal passive), letter and note
  conventions.
- **16. Negation beyond `ne… pas`** — `ne… que`, `ne… plus`, `personne` and
  `rien` as subject, dropped `ne` in speech.
- **6. Adjectives — agreement and position** — including the ones whose meaning
  changes by position (`un ancien collègue` vs `un collègue ancien`).
- **7. Comparatives and superlatives** — `plus/moins/aussi… que`, `meilleur` vs
  `mieux`, `le plus`.

**Remaining**

- **9. Future and conditional** — `futur proche` vs `futur simple`, conditional
  for politeness and hypothesis.
- **11. The subjunctive** — triggers, formation, and when a native sidesteps it.
  *15 drill items exist.*
- **12. Pronominal and reflexive verbs** — including past agreement.
  *20 drill items exist.*
- **13. Infinitive vs subjunctive after a verb** — `je veux que` vs `j'espère
  que` vs `je pense que`.
- **2. Telling the time** — conversational vs the 24-hour clock used on every
  STIB screen and official notice.
- **15b. Present tense: the three groups and the irregulars that recur.**
  *Well covered by the 120-item conjugation bank; lowest priority.*

## Level pairing

Grammar topics are not all A2 material, so each is built at the two levels that
suit it rather than a forced A2/B1 pair — see the table above. The unlock rule
is the same as Conversations: topics in order, and the upper level of a topic
opens when its lower level is finished.
